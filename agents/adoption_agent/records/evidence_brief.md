# Fixed evidence brief

Checked against original sources on October 27, 2026. This is a selective source review, not a comprehensive legal or market assessment. Preserve this brief unchanged across cases. Distinguish current diffusion evidence from proof of application benefit.

E1. NIST, AI 600-1, July 2024, section 2.2 and risk overview. Generative systems can produce plausible incorrect information; data privacy and human over-reliance are relevant risks. Voluntary risk guidance, not bank-specific performance evidence or legal approval. https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf

E2. Bank of England and FCA, Artificial intelligence in UK financial services 2026, published October 26, 2026, executive summary and sections 1.2 and 2. Survey received 196 responses; 105 completed the full questionnaire. Overall 86% reported AI use. GenAI accounted for 56% of reported AI systems. This establishes use among respondents, not successful US bank risk-intake deployments. Changed definitions and respondent populations limit comparison with 2024. https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/report/2026/artificial-intelligence-in-uk-financial-services-2026

E3. FINRA Regulatory Notice 24-09, June 27, 2024. Existing rules apply to member firms using GenAI; oversight must address relevant technology risks. The hypothetical bank's FINRA membership and legal structure are unknown. Use conditionally for a member firm or affiliate; do not assert universal applicability. https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/24-09

E4. Federal Reserve SR 26-2 and attached revised guidance, April 17, 2026. Generally most relevant above $30 billion in assets; generative and agentic AI are expressly outside the attachment's scope. Do not present SR 26-2 as directly governing this LLM. Governance of excluded systems still requires organizational consideration. The bank's size and regulator are unknown. https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr2602.htm and https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602a1.pdf
